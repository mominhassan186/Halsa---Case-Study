01 — OVERVIEW

Designing a connected healthcare ecosystem for patients and physicians.

Halsa is envisioned as a connected healthcare ecosystem designed to bring patients, physicians, caregivers, and critical health information into one connected experience.

The Halsa Vision

Modern medical care demands high precision, yet everyday healthcare interactions remain siloed across incompatible portals, isolated appointment tools, and disconnected physical records.

Halsa bridges this division by creating a shared digital ecosystem—unifying personal health records, clinical scheduling, intelligent triage, and consultation workflows into one coherent, human-centered experience.

“Healthcare should feel connected — not fragmented.”

The Core Challenge

Patients often manage chronic conditions and critical appointments under stress with fragmented information. Meanwhile, physicians face cognitive overload from scattered documentation and rigid interfaces.

The challenge was to design a single ecosystem that respects these opposing realities: providing reassurance, clarity, and agency for patients, while delivering speed, context, and diagnostic density for clinicians.

Balancing: Calm Agency ↔ Clinical Precision
02 — THE PROBLEM

Healthcare information is everywhere. The context is not.

Patients and doctors rarely suffer from a lack of medical data. The genuine breakdown occurs in synthesis: vital indicators, treatment history, and action plans are scattered across disconnected touchpoints, isolating data from the real-time context needed to make confident decisions.

Key Problems

01 · FRAGMENTATION

Disconnected Information

Health information lives across appointments, records, personal devices, and disparate clinics, obscuring the complete clinical picture.

02 · COMPLEXITY

Cognitive Load

Healthcare interfaces are chronically overloaded with dense, jargon-heavy information that users must interpret rapidly under stress or time limits.

03 · FRICTION

High-Stress Moments

In urgent situations, complex navigation, unclear menus, and multi-step confirmation screens create dangerous friction when speed is vital.

The Core Design Challenge

“How might we create a healthcare experience that makes complex information feel connected, understandable, and actionable?”

03 — UNDERSTANDING THE USERS

Healthcare is not one experience.

Healthcare interfaces cannot rely on a one-size-fits-all model. A patient checking lab results late at night requires reassurance and plain language. A physician running back-to-back consultations needs keyboard shortcuts, scannable data visualization, and immediate diagnostic context.

User Group Core Needs Primary Motivation
Patient Clarity, control, reassurance, straightforward access to records Understanding health status without medical jargon
Physician Diagnostic context, clinical speed, longitudinal histories, frictionless charting Rapid clinical synthesis and uninterrupted focus on care
Caregiver Visibility, delegated control, and transparency Acting confidently on behalf of a dependent
PERSONA 01 Primary User

Patient

“Understand and manage personal health with minimal effort.”

Core Needs:
  • Quick access to vital health indicators & dosage schedules
  • Straightforward appointment booking & real-time visibility
  • Plain-language health records without jargon
  • Clear triage guidance when symptoms feel ambiguous
  • Rapid, friction-free assistance in urgent situations
Pain Points:
  • Medical details are scattered across disparate portals
  • Test results lack context and provoke unnecessary anxiety
  • Scheduling appointments requires tedious back-and-forth
  • Urgent situations demand too many micro-decisions
UX Priority Clarity + Reassurance + Control
PERSONA 02 Healthcare Professional

Physician

“Understand patient context and manage interactions efficiently.”

Core Needs:
  • Instant synthesis of patient histories prior to consults
  • Structured, distraction-free consultation workflows
  • Longitudinal health trends & reliable medication logs
  • Rapid follow-up scheduling & e-prescriptions
  • High-density, scannable clinical data layouts
Pain Points:
  • Charting systems bury critical historical warnings
  • Clunky interfaces slow face-to-face patient engagement
  • Asynchronous patient messages lack structured triage
  • Context switching between EHR tabs induces fatigue
UX Priority Context + Efficiency + Accuracy
Caregiver Perspective

Secondary Ecosystem Persona

Caregivers represent a vital secondary tier in the healthcare ecosystem. Rather than requiring distinct software, they need delegated access, visibility, and confidence when coordinating appointments, tracking medication adherence, or navigating emergencies on behalf of a dependent or elderly family member.

Key Friction Points Solved
• Information Discovery: Eliminates hunting through multi-level folders.
• Data Interpretation: Replaces raw values with normal reference ranges.
• Task Switching: Combines booking, records, and calls in one tool.
• Emergency Friction: Removes multi-step forms when seconds matter.

06 — THE DESIGN SOLUTION

Turning the strategy into a connected patient experience.

The patient portal brings daily health tracking, records access, scheduling, and triage guidance into a singular, cohesive environment engineered for calm and control.

01 · Daily Monitoring

Health Overview

Gives users an immediate, digestible snapshot of their current well-being.

  • Prioritized daily medication tracker with one-tap confirmation
  • Real-time vital metrics (heart rate, activity, sleep) integrated cleanly
  • Personalized alerts highlighting pending health tasks
02 · Scheduling

Appointments

Make upcoming care easier to see, manage, and act on without scheduling anxiety.

  • Chronological view of past visits and upcoming consultations
  • Context cards with clinic locations, doctor credentials, and prep
  • Direct rescheduling, cancellations, and virtual check-in
03 · Clinical Records

Health Records

Keep important health information accessible without overwhelming the user.

  • Diagnostic lab reports translated with visual normal-range indicators
  • Longitudinal vaccination histories, clinical notes, and summaries
  • Secure sharing controls for instant record exports
04 · Intelligent Care

AI / Triage

Provide a guided path when users are unsure what to do next.

  • Natural language symptom input with structured follow-up prompts
  • Algorithmic urgency scoring differentiating routine from emergency
  • Direct routing into teleconsultation, urgent care, or home care
Critical Path High-Stress UX

Emergency Experience

Designed strictly for high-stress scenarios where every second matters. Features a dedicated, one-tap Emergency SOS banner accessible system-wide, direct dial to local emergency services paired with automatic location sharing, and instant lockscreen Medical ID display (blood type, allergies, emergency contacts).

10 — REFLECTION

Designing healthcare means designing for moments that matter.

Design Decisions

  • Prioritize information hierarchy: Key vitals and alerts take precedence over secondary administrative functions.
  • Separate patient and physician workflows: Avoid compromise interfaces by purpose-building each view for its cognitive load.
  • Reduce cognitive load: Medical data structured into modular cards and reference range meters.
  • Design for high-stress moments: Direct SOS and triage paths bypass deep menu hierarchies entirely.
  • Build consistency across the ecosystem: Unified medical terminology keeps patient inputs in sync with clinician charts.

Key Takeaways

01

Healthcare UX is about context, not just information. Providing medical data without plain-language context creates anxiety. Interface structure determines whether information empowers or overwhelms.

02

Different users need different experiences built on connected data. Patients require reassurance and clarity; doctors need clinical density and speed. A unified ecosystem can serve both by decoupling presentation.

03

In high-stress moments, clarity becomes a core interaction. In urgent health scenarios, intuitive navigation and direct paths are essential lifelines.

“Halsa explores what healthcare could feel like when the experience is designed around people, context, and connection.”
